Generic vs brand-name drugs

A generic can cost a fraction of the brand-name price. This guide explains what a generic is, why it is cheaper, when it is a safe switch — and when it is not.

Important: Informational only — not medical advice. Always consult your pharmacist or doctor about medicines, doses, interactions and symptoms.

What is a generic drug?

When a brand-name drug's patent ends, other companies may make the same medicine. A generic contains the same active ingredient, in the same strength and form, and must meet the same quality standards. The inactive ingredients — fillers, colors, coatings — can differ, which is why the tablet may look different.

Why are generics cheaper?

The original company spent years and large sums on research, trials and marketing. Generic makers do not repeat that work, so they can sell the same active ingredient for much less. The price difference is real: our comparison tool shows a typical example where a $120 brand costs $35 as a generic — about 71% less.

Are generics as good?

Regulators require generics to work the same way in the body as the brand. For most medicines, most people notice no difference. A small number of medicines — often called narrow therapeutic index drugs, such as some thyroid or anti-seizure medicines — need extra care, because small changes in blood levels matter. Your doctor or pharmacist will tell you if your medicine is one of them.

When to be careful

SituationWhat to do
You have allergiesCheck the inactive ingredients with your pharmacist
You take a narrow therapeutic index drugDo not switch without your doctor's approval
You feel different after switchingTell your pharmacist or doctor promptly
You take many medicinesAsk for a full medication review

How to ask

  1. Ask your pharmacist: 'Is there a generic for this medicine?'
  2. Ask about the price difference — including your insurance copay.
  3. Ask whether the generic suits your allergies and other medicines.
  4. If you switch, note how you feel for the first two weeks and report anything unusual.
