What to ask your pharmacist

Pharmacists are medicine experts — and their advice is free. These ten questions cover side effects, food, other medicines and missed doses. Take this list to your next visit.

Important: Informational only — not medical advice. Always consult your pharmacist or doctor about medicines, doses, interactions and symptoms.

The ten questions

  1. What is this medicine for? — Know the purpose, not just the name.
  2. How and when should I take it? — With food? Morning or evening?
  3. What are the common side effects? — And which ones need urgent attention?
  4. What should I do if I miss a dose? — Take it late, skip it, or double up? Never guess.
  5. Can I take it with my other medicines? — Include vitamins, supplements and herbal products.
  6. Can I drink alcohol while taking it? — Some combinations are dangerous.
  7. Is there a cheaper generic? — Often yes; ask about the price difference.
  8. How should I store it? — Fridge, cupboard, or away from light?
  9. How long should I take it? — Until the bottle is empty, or longer?
  10. When should I come back? — For a refill, a check-up or a medication review?

How to prepare for the conversation

Bring a list of everything you take — prescription medicines, over-the-counter products, vitamins and supplements — with doses. Mention allergies, pregnancy or plans to become pregnant, and any side effects you have noticed. If you take many medicines, ask for a medication review: the pharmacist will check the whole list together for interactions and duplicates.

After the visit

Write down the answers, especially anything about missed doses and side effects. If you start feeling different after a new medicine, note when it started and call the pharmacy. Pharmacists would rather answer a small question early than deal with a problem later.
