Storing medicines safely at home

Heat, humidity and light can weaken medicines — and a bottle in the wrong place can be dangerous for children. This guide covers where to store medicines and how to dispose of old ones.

Important: Informational only — not medical advice. Always consult your pharmacist or doctor about medicines, doses, interactions and symptoms.

The bathroom cabinet is not always best

Despite the name, the bathroom medicine cabinet is often the worst place for medicines. Showers create heat and steam, and humidity can damage tablets and capsules. A better spot is a cool, dry cupboard in a bedroom or kitchen — away from the stove, the sink and direct sunlight. Always follow the storage instructions on the label: some medicines must be refrigerated, and a few must be kept frozen.

Keep medicines in their original containers

Original containers protect medicines from light and moisture and carry the label with the dose and expiry date. Do not mix different medicines in one bottle, and do not remove the label. Pill organizers are useful for a week at a time, but keep the original packs so you can check the expiry date and instructions.

Children and pets

Store all medicines — including vitamins, supplements and creams — up high and out of sight, ideally in a locked box or a high cupboard. Child-resistant caps help, but they are not child-proof: a determined child can open many of them. Never call medicine 'candy' to a child, and never take your own medicine in front of children if you can avoid it.

What heat and cold do

ConditionRiskWhat to do
Heat (above 25–30 °C)Medicine weakens fasterMove to a cooler room; never leave in a car
HumidityTablets crumble or stickKeep the lid closed; avoid the bathroom
FreezingSome liquids separate or breakOnly freeze if the label says so
SunlightSome medicines break downKeep in the original opaque container

Disposing of old medicines

Check expiry dates twice a year — for example when the clocks change. Take expired or unused medicines to a pharmacy take-back program if one is available. Do not flush medicines unless the label specifically instructs it, and do not throw them in the trash unless your local guidance says to.
